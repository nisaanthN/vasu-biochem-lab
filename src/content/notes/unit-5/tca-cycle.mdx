## What the TCA cycle does

After glycolysis, pyruvate enters the mitochondrion and is decarboxylated to **acetyl-CoA** by pyruvate dehydrogenase (PDH). The TCA cycle (also known as the citric acid cycle or Krebs cycle) **completely oxidises the 2-carbon acetyl group** to two CO₂, harvesting the bond energy as NADH, FADH₂, and one GTP per turn.

It's *the* metabolic hub: amino acids, fatty acids, and ketone bodies all feed in; biosynthesis of amino acids, haem, and gluconeogenesis branch off.

## The PDH gateway

Pyruvate (3C, cytosolic) → Acetyl-CoA (2C, mitochondrial) + CO₂ + NADH.

PDH is a giant multi-enzyme complex with three components and five cofactors:

- E1 (pyruvate dehydrogenase) — uses TPP (vitamin B1).
- E2 (dihydrolipoyl transacetylase) — uses lipoic acid and CoA.
- E3 (dihydrolipoyl dehydrogenase) — uses FAD and NAD⁺.

**Vitamin connection**: PDH needs **B1, B2, B3, B5, and lipoic acid**. B1 (thiamine) deficiency hits PDH first — explains the neurologic features of beriberi and Wernicke encephalopathy (in chronic alcoholism — alcohol disrupts thiamine absorption and storage).

PDH is inhibited by its products (NADH, acetyl-CoA) and is also covalently regulated by phosphorylation. Insulin activates the phosphatase that dephosphorylates and turns PDH on; glucagon does the opposite.

## The 8 steps

```
        Acetyl-CoA (2C)  +  Oxaloacetate (4C)
                 │ Citrate synthase
                 ▼
         Citrate (6C)  ─aconitase─►  Isocitrate (6C)
                                          │ Isocitrate DH  → CO₂ + NADH
                                          ▼
                                   α-Ketoglutarate (5C)
                                          │ α-KG DH complex → CO₂ + NADH
                                          ▼
                                   Succinyl-CoA (4C)
                                          │ Succinyl-CoA synthetase → GTP
                                          ▼
                                    Succinate (4C)
                                          │ Succinate DH → FADH₂  (= Complex II of ETC)
                                          ▼
                                     Fumarate (4C)
                                          │ Fumarase + H₂O
                                          ▼
                                      Malate (4C)
                                          │ Malate DH → NADH
                                          ▼
                                   Oxaloacetate (4C)  ◄── back to top
```

### One-line per enzyme

1. **Citrate synthase** — condensation. Inhibited by citrate, ATP, NADH, succinyl-CoA.
2. **Aconitase** — isomerisation (citrate → isocitrate via cis-aconitate).
3. **Isocitrate dehydrogenase** — oxidative decarboxylation. **Rate-limiting step**. Activated by ADP, Ca²⁺. Inhibited by ATP, NADH.
4. **α-Ketoglutarate dehydrogenase** — oxidative decarboxylation. Same five-cofactor mechanism as PDH (B1, B2, B3, B5, lipoic acid).
5. **Succinyl-CoA synthetase** — substrate-level phosphorylation → GTP (or ATP).
6. **Succinate dehydrogenase** — the only TCA enzyme bound to the inner mitochondrial membrane (it's **Complex II** of the ETC). Produces FADH₂.
7. **Fumarase** — hydration.
8. **Malate dehydrogenase** — final oxidation to oxaloacetate.

## Energy yield per acetyl-CoA

| Step | Carrier produced | ATP equivalents |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Isocitrate DH | 1 NADH | 2.5 |
| α-KG DH | 1 NADH | 2.5 |
| Succinyl-CoA synthetase | 1 GTP | 1 |
| Succinate DH | 1 FADH₂ | 1.5 |
| Malate DH | 1 NADH | 2.5 |
| **Total** | | **10 ATP** |

Per glucose, **two turns of the TCA cycle**, so **20 ATP** from TCA alone (plus ~5 from PDH × 2, plus ~7 from glycolysis = ~32 total).

## Three regulatory enzymes

PDH, isocitrate dehydrogenase, and α-KG dehydrogenase — all inhibited by their NADH/ATP products and activated by their ADP/AMP/Ca²⁺ substrates. The cycle accelerates when energy demand is high and brakes when ATP is plentiful.

<Callout variant="info" title="Why Ca²⁺ activates the TCA cycle">
Muscle contraction releases Ca²⁺ into the cytosol — and some into the mitochondrial matrix. Ca²⁺ activates PDH phosphatase (turning PDH on), and also directly stimulates isocitrate DH and α-KG DH. The contracting muscle simultaneously signals "make more ATP" and "demand more ATP". Elegant coupling.
</Callout>

## Anaplerotic reactions — refilling the cycle

If TCA intermediates are drained off for biosynthesis (e.g., α-ketoglutarate to glutamate, succinyl-CoA to haem, oxaloacetate to aspartate), the cycle can't keep turning. **Anaplerotic** reactions refill them:

- **Pyruvate carboxylase**: pyruvate + CO₂ + ATP → oxaloacetate. Activated by acetyl-CoA. Major anaplerotic source in liver and a key gluconeogenic entry.
- **Glutamate dehydrogenase**: glutamate → α-KG (during amino acid catabolism).
- **Methylmalonyl-CoA mutase**: feeds odd-chain fatty acid β-oxidation products into succinyl-CoA (B12-dependent).

## Metabolic disorders worth knowing

### Diabetes mellitus

Type 1: autoimmune destruction of β-cells → no insulin.
Type 2: peripheral insulin resistance + relative insulin deficiency.

The biochemistry that matters:

- Without insulin, GLUT4 doesn't translocate to membrane; muscle and adipose can't take up glucose → **hyperglycemia**.
- Liver runs gluconeogenesis flat-out → makes hyperglycemia worse.
- Adipose lipolysis is uncontrolled → free fatty acids → liver makes ketone bodies → **diabetic ketoacidosis** (DKA) in T1.
- Chronic hyperglycemia → non-enzymatic glycation of proteins → HbA1c (the diagnostic marker), basement-membrane thickening (microvascular complications), advanced glycation end-products (AGEs).

Drug classes target each of these mechanisms: metformin (suppresses hepatic gluconeogenesis), sulfonylureas (insulin secretion), GLP-1 agonists (insulin secretion + central appetite suppression), SGLT2 inhibitors (renal glucose excretion).

### Phenylketonuria (PKU)

Autosomal recessive deficiency of **phenylalanine hydroxylase** — the enzyme that converts phenylalanine to tyrosine. Phenylalanine builds up; it's transaminated to phenylpyruvate (the "ketone" the name refers to).

- **Untreated**: severe intellectual disability, seizures, light skin and hair pigmentation (tyrosine is melanin precursor).
- **Treatment**: lifelong low-phenylalanine diet, tyrosine supplementation. Sapropterin (BH4 analogue) for some forms.
- **Screening**: heel-prick Guthrie test at 48-72 hours of life.

Aspartame is hydrolysed to aspartate + phenylalanine + methanol — hence "PHENYLKETONURICS: contains phenylalanine" labels on diet sodas.

### Gout

Hyperuricemia → monosodium urate crystal deposition in joints (acutely painful arthritis) and tissues (tophi).

The biochemistry:

- **Purines** (A, G) are catabolised through hypoxanthine → xanthine → **uric acid** by **xanthine oxidase**.
- Excessive nucleic acid breakdown (tumor lysis syndrome), high-purine diet (red meat, seafood), or impaired renal excretion → urate accumulation.
- **Lesch-Nyhan syndrome**: HGPRT deficiency → can't salvage purines → degraded to urate → severe juvenile gout + neurologic features.

Drug classes:

- **Allopurinol, febuxostat**: xanthine oxidase inhibitors (decrease production).
- **Probenecid**: uricosuric (increase renal excretion).
- **Colchicine, NSAIDs**: anti-inflammatory for acute attacks.
- **Pegloticase**: recombinant uricase for refractory disease (humans lack uricase, which is why we get gout when other mammals don't).
