## Why we classify enzymes

Without a system, "trypsin", "ATPase", and "hexokinase" are arbitrary names. The IUBMB system gives every enzyme a four-number EC code (`EC a.b.c.d`) that pinpoints its **reaction type, substrate, and bond involved**. Pharmacy drug targets, diagnostic markers, and metabolic pathways all reference EC classes — so learning to decode the first digit alone unlocks most of the syllabus.

## The six classes

<KeyPoint>
Mnemonic: **Over The Hill, Like It Loves** — Oxidoreductases, Transferases, Hydrolases, Lyases, Isomerases, Ligases.
</KeyPoint>

### EC 1 — Oxidoreductases

Catalyse **oxidation–reduction** (electron transfer). Always need a coenzyme (NAD⁺/NADH, NADP⁺/NADPH, FAD/FADH₂) or a metal as the electron acceptor or donor.

Sub-classes: dehydrogenases (transfer H), oxidases (use O₂, no H₂O₂), peroxidases (use H₂O₂), reductases, oxygenases.

**Examples in pharmacy**:
- Alcohol dehydrogenase (target of disulfiram in alcohol-use disorder)
- Lactate dehydrogenase (LDH — myocardial-infarction biomarker)
- HMG-CoA reductase (target of all statins)
- Xanthine oxidase (target of allopurinol in gout)

### EC 2 — Transferases

Transfer a chemical group (-CH₃, -NH₂, -CHO, phosphate, glucose) **from one substrate to another**.

Sub-classes: methyltransferases, aminotransferases (transaminases), kinases (phosphate transfer from ATP), phosphorylases (phosphate transfer using inorganic P).

**Examples**:
- AST (aspartate aminotransferase) — liver-function test, MI biomarker
- ALT (alanine aminotransferase) — liver-function test
- Hexokinase, glucokinase, pyruvate kinase — glycolytic steps
- Catechol-O-methyltransferase (COMT — target of entacapone in Parkinson's)
- Thiopurine S-methyltransferase (TPMT — pharmacogenomic concern for azathioprine)

### EC 3 — Hydrolases

Catalyse **bond cleavage with insertion of water** (no coenzyme needed). The most heterogeneous class.

Sub-classes: esterases (cleave esters), peptidases / proteases (peptide bonds), glycosidases (glycosidic bonds), nucleases (phosphodiester), lipases, phosphatases.

**Examples**:
- Pepsin, trypsin, chymotrypsin — digestive proteases
- Acetylcholinesterase — target of neostigmine, donepezil, organophosphates
- α-amylase (salivary, pancreatic) — starch digestion
- ATPase (e.g., Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase) — target of cardiac glycosides like digoxin
- Alkaline phosphatase — biomarker for bone disease, cholestasis

### EC 4 — Lyases

Cleave bonds **without hydrolysis or oxidation**, often forming a new double bond or ring. Equivalently, the reverse reactions *add* groups across double bonds.

Sub-classes: decarboxylases (remove -COOH as CO₂), dehydratases (remove water), aldolases.

**Examples**:
- Aldolase (glycolysis step 4)
- Fumarase (TCA cycle)
- Histidine decarboxylase → histamine
- DOPA decarboxylase (also called aromatic-L-amino-acid decarboxylase — target of carbidopa)
- Pyruvate decarboxylase (yeast fermentation; not in human glycolysis)

### EC 5 — Isomerases

**Intramolecular rearrangements**: substrate and product have the same atoms in different arrangements.

Sub-classes: racemases (D↔L), epimerases, cis-trans isomerases, mutases (move a group within the molecule).

**Examples**:
- Phosphohexose isomerase (glycolysis step 2: G6P ↔ F6P)
- Phosphoglycerate mutase (glycolysis step 8)
- Triose phosphate isomerase (glycolysis step 5)
- Topoisomerase (DNA — target of quinolones, etoposide, irinotecan)

### EC 6 — Ligases (synthetases)

**Join two molecules** with **simultaneous ATP hydrolysis** as the energy source.

Sub-classes: synthetases that form C–O, C–S, C–N, or C–C bonds.

**Examples**:
- DNA ligase (joins Okazaki fragments)
- Aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases (charge tRNAs for translation)
- Pyruvate carboxylase (gluconeogenesis — converts pyruvate to oxaloacetate, ATP-dependent)
- Acetyl-CoA carboxylase (fatty-acid synthesis initiation)

<Callout variant="info" title="Distinguish ligase from kinase">
A kinase **transfers** a phosphate; it's a transferase (class 2). A ligase **joins two molecules** and uses ATP to drive the energetically uphill reaction. The energy source is the same (ATP) — the operation is different.
</Callout>

## Decoding an EC number

`EC 1.1.1.27` — lactate dehydrogenase. Reading left to right:

| Digit | Meaning | Value for LDH |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Class | 1 (oxidoreductase) |
| 2 | Sub-class (group transferred / acted on) | 1 (acts on CHOH group) |
| 3 | Sub-sub-class (acceptor) | 1 (uses NAD⁺/NADP⁺ as acceptor) |
| 4 | Serial number within the sub-sub-class | 27 |

You only need to memorise the **first digit** for any common enzyme — that's the level questions test.

## Special enzyme categories worth knowing

- **Holoenzyme** = apoenzyme (protein) + cofactor (metal ion or coenzyme).
- **Isoenzymes (isozymes)** = different proteins catalysing the same reaction, often with different kinetics. LDH-1 to LDH-5 in different tissues; CPK-MM (muscle), CPK-MB (heart), CPK-BB (brain).
- **Zymogens (proenzymes)** = inactive precursors activated by limited proteolysis. Pepsinogen → pepsin; chymotrypsinogen → chymotrypsin; all clotting factors.
- **Monomeric vs allosteric** = follow MM kinetics vs sigmoidal kinetics (see the Michaelis-Menten note).
- **Coenzymes** = organic cofactors, often vitamin-derived. NAD⁺/NADP⁺ (niacin), FAD/FMN (riboflavin), CoA (pantothenic acid), PLP (B6), TPP (B1), THF (folate), biotin (B7), B12 (cobalamin).

<Mnemonic phrase="Statin Mnemonic — HMG-CoA reductase is class 1 because it REDUCES HMG-CoA to mevalonate, the rate-limiting step of cholesterol synthesis.">
The drug class is named after the enzyme it inhibits. Atorvastatin, rosuvastatin, simvastatin all bind the HMG-CoA reductase active site, lowering cholesterol synthesis.
</Mnemonic>
